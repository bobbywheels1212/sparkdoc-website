Lost Power in the Bathroom, Garage, or Outside?

The bathroom outlets are dead. The garage is dead. The plug on the outside wall is dead. The rest of the house is fine. That is usually one GFCI, not three separate problems. Spark Doc Electric is based in Olathe and serves Johnson County KS, Jackson County MO, and Wyandotte County KS.

Quick answer

A GFCI is the outlet with TEST and RESET. It can feed other outlets that have no buttons. When it trips, the bathroom, the garage, and the outside can all go dead together. The GFCI you need to reset is often in the garage, not in the room that lost power. It can also be a breaker in the panel with a test button.

They Are Wired Together on Purpose

One GFCI can protect a string of ordinary outlets. Those other outlets look normal. No buttons. They are just along for the ride. Electricians call that the load side. When the GFCI trips, every outlet wired after it goes dead at the same time.

On a lot of houses the GFCI that protects the bathroom is not in the bathroom. It is in the garage. Or outside, under a cover. Or in the other bathroom. The dead outlet you are staring at may not be the one that can turn the power back on.

Unplug first

A wet extension cord, a pressure washer, holiday lights, or a fridge in the garage is a common reason the GFCI opened. Unplug what is on the dead outlets before you reset it.

It Might Be a Breaker, Not an Outlet

Some houses have no GFCI outlet at all on that run. The protection is a GFCI breaker in the panel. Open the panel door. Do not take the metal cover off.

Look for a breaker with a small test button, or one marked GFCI. That button is a test. It is not a reset. If the handle is sitting off, or halfway between on and off, push it firmly all the way off, then all the way on.

  • Test button on the breaker trips it. It does not bring the power back.
  • Reset means handle all the way off, then all the way on.
  • Trips again immediately? Leave it off and call. Do not keep forcing it on.

What You Can Check

  1. Unplug whatever is plugged into the dead outlets, especially anything that got wet.
  2. Look at the dead outlet. If it has TEST and RESET, press RESET firmly until it clicks.
  3. No buttons? Check the garage, both bathrooms, the outside outlets, the basement, the laundry, and the kitchen. Press RESET on the first one that has buttons.
  4. Still dead? Open the panel door only. Find the breaker with a test button or a GFCI mark. Push the handle all the way off, then on.
  5. When the power comes back, press TEST once. The outlets should die. Press RESET and they should come back. If TEST does nothing, the outlet can still be live and it is not protecting you.
  6. If it trips again the second you reset it, stop. Something on that run is leaking current, or the GFCI is worn out.

A single dead outlet is a different call. So is a breaker that keeps tripping with no GFCI button on it.

Why These Exist

A normal breaker watches for too much current. A short, or an overload. It does not notice a small amount of current that left on the hot wire and did not come back on the neutral.

That missing current found another path. Water. A person. A GFCI is looking for that, a few thousandths of an amp, and it opens fast.

A missing GFCI does not make the outlet stop working. That is why people miss it. The hair dryer, the garage freezer, and the outdoor cord still run. The protection just is not there.

A GFCI that will not trip when you press TEST is the same hole. It looks fine. It is not doing the job.

Where You Need Them

You need that protection anywhere you and water can meet the wiring.

  • Bathrooms
  • The garage
  • Outside
  • Kitchen counters
  • The laundry
  • An unfinished basement or a crawl space
  • Within a few feet of a sink

A lot of older Olathe houses still have plain outlets in those spots. The outlet works. The protection was never there. If you want those outlets protected, that is a GFCI repair and install, not another reset.

Common Questions

Bathroom, Garage, and Outside Outlets

Why did my bathroom, garage, and outside outlets die at the same time?
One GFCI can feed all of them. The outlet with the TEST and RESET buttons protects the ordinary outlets wired after it. When it trips, every outlet on that chain goes dead together.
The dead outlet has no TEST or RESET button. Where do I look?
Check the garage, both bathrooms, the outside outlets, the basement, the laundry, and the kitchen. Press RESET on the first outlet you find that has those buttons.
What if the GFCI is a breaker in the panel?
Look for a breaker with a test button, or one marked GFCI. The button tests it. It does not reset it. If the handle is off or in the middle, push it all the way off, then all the way on. Do not remove the panel cover.
Why are GFCIs important?
A regular breaker trips on a short or an overload. A GFCI trips when a small amount of current leaves on the hot wire and does not come back on the neutral. That missing current may be going through water or a person. Bathrooms, garages, outdoors, kitchen counters, laundry, unfinished basements, and spots near a sink need that protection.
When should I stop resetting it and call?
Stop if it will not reset, if it trips again right away, if TEST does nothing, or if the device is hot or browned. Call (913) 390-3780 or book online.
