Federal Pacific Stab-Lok Panels: Risks and Replacement

Federal Pacific Stab-Lok panels show up in older Johnson County houses, including 1960s through 1980s homes in Olathe. The concern is specific: breakers that may not trip. This is the full guide, and the recommendation is replacement.

Quick answer

If the panel door says Federal Pacific, FPE, or Stab-Lok, plan on replacing the panel. Breaker swaps are not a reliable fix. This page is only about Stab-Lok. Zinsco is a different panel, covered in its own guide.

What a Stab-Lok panel is

Federal Pacific Electric sold Stab-Lok breaker panels widely from the 1950s into the 1980s. You will still find them in Johnson County houses from that era, including older parts of Olathe. The name is on the label inside the door, and often on the breakers. Open the door to look. Do not remove the metal cover over the breakers.

A breaker's job is simple. When a circuit overloads or short-circuits, the breaker trips and shuts the power off. Testing tied to the history of these breakers, including the Consumer Product Safety Commission's investigation in the early 1980s, found Stab-Lok breakers that did not trip when they should. The investigation closed without a mandatory recall of every panel already installed in a house. The test history is why electricians still recommend replacement.

That is a specific failure, not a scare story. Plenty of these panels have sat quietly for decades. Quiet does not mean the breaker will open the next time a lamp cord shorts. The protection is the part you cannot see working until the day you need it.

What we recommend, and what we do not

We recommend replacing the panel. We do not recommend filling it with "replacement" Stab-Lok breakers and calling it done. Those breakers still land on the same bus, in the same design. If the house is on 100 amps and you are adding an EV charger, a generator, or modern HVAC, the replacement can be a 200-amp upgrade in the same project. If the service size is already enough, we replace the panel at the same amperage. More amps are not the product if the problem is the can.

  • Confirm the label on site. A photo of the door is a fine start.
  • Decide same-size replacement versus a capacity upgrade.
  • Pull the permit. In Olathe that inspection goes through the City of Olathe.
  • Schedule a daytime outage, land every circuit, and meet the inspector.
Do not do this yourself

Do not remove the dead front, and do not swap breakers in a live Stab-Lok panel. Take a photo of the label and schedule a system assessment.

Insurance, home sales, and the neighbor's panel

Many insurers ask about Federal Pacific panels when a house is sold or a policy is renewed. Some require replacement before they will cover the house. Not every carrier uses the same rule, and a letter from your company is the document that matters, not a rumor. If you are buying in Olathe or elsewhere in Johnson County and the report says Federal Pacific, treat replacement as part of the plan. The short overview of both Federal Pacific and Zinsco is still on the site if you want the one-page version. Zinsco has its own failure story and its own page.

Common Questions

Federal Pacific Stab-Lok questions

Are Federal Pacific Stab-Lok panels dangerous?
They have a documented history of breakers that can fail to trip when they should. That does not mean every Stab-Lok panel starts a fire. It does mean the breaker may not do the one job it exists to do. Replacement is the usual recommendation from electricians and from many insurance companies.
Were Stab-Lok panels recalled?
There was not a single recall that removed every installed Stab-Lok panel from homes. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission investigated the breakers in the early 1980s, and that case closed without a mandatory recall of the panels already in houses. Testing from that history found breakers that did not trip. That is why replacement is still recommended.
Can I replace the breakers and keep the panel?
Swapping breakers is not a reliable fix. The bus and the breaker design are the problem, and a breaker made for that panel does not turn it into a modern panel. The recommendation is to replace the panel. If the house also needs more capacity, that can be a 100-amp to 200-amp upgrade in the same project.
How do I tell if I have a Federal Pacific panel?
Open the panel door only. Do not remove the metal cover over the breakers. Look for the words Federal Pacific, FPE, or Stab-Lok on the label or the breakers. A photo of that label is enough for us to confirm it. If you are unsure, schedule a system assessment.
Do I need a permit to replace one in Olathe?
Yes. A panel change in Olathe needs a permit and an inspection through the City of Olathe. Spark Doc Electric pulls the permit and meets the inspector. The same is true in most other Kansas City metro cities.
