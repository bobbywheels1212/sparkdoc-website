Signs Your Home Needs a Service or Panel Upgrade

Homeowners usually notice the symptoms before they know the name of the job: breakers that trip, a panel with no spaces, or a project that needs a new 240-volt circuit. Here is how those signs map to a repair, a replacement, or a real service upgrade.

Quick answer

A panel upgrade is about capacity and condition, not a bigger breaker. Signs include a 100-amp service asked to run a modern house, breakers that trip under ordinary combinations of loads, no spaces left, or a project such as an EV charger or a generator that the panel cannot accept. Heat, buzzing, or a burning smell is a call first, not an automatic sales pitch for more amps.

Signs the service is behind the house

  • The main label says 100 amps, and the house now has central air, an electric range, a dryer, and more.
  • Breakers trip when normal things run together, such as the microwave and the air conditioner, and it happens more than once.
  • The panel is full. Every space is used, or the spaces are full of thin tandem breakers and there is nowhere for a new circuit.
  • You want an EV charger, a hot tub, a workshop, a tankless electric water heater, or a generator, and the panel has no room or no capacity.
  • An insurer, an inspector, or a remodeler has already written that the service is undersized.

One trip after you know you overloaded a circuit is the breaker working. Unplug the extra load. A pattern of trips, with ordinary use, is the service or that circuit telling you something. The longer note on a breaker that will not stay on is breaker keeps tripping.

Signs that are not "just add amps"

A warm panel, a buzzing sound, a burning smell, or scorch marks are a reason to stop and call. The cause might be a loose connection. Putting in a larger main does not repair a loose lug, and putting a larger breaker on a small wire makes the wire the fuse. Do not open the panel cover to look. Do not swap in a bigger breaker because the old one "nuisance trips."

Lights that dim or brighten across several rooms when a motor starts can be a loose neutral. That is a repair, and it can matter the same day. It can also be a service that is near its limit. Those two look similar from the sofa. They are not diagnosed from a blog post.

Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels are a replacement even when the amperage number looks fine. Brand first, then capacity. Read Stab-Lok and Zinsco if the label matches.

What the assessment decides

We look at the service size, the spaces, the condition of the bus and the breakers, and the load you want to add. Then we say one of three things: the panel is fine and you need a dedicated circuit, the panel should be replaced at the same size, or the house needs a 100-amp to 200-amp panel upgrade. An EV charger or a generator often forces that conversation, because both need space and capacity.

Common Questions

Panel upgrade questions

How do I know the service is too small?
The usual signs are a 100-amp panel in a house with modern electric loads, breakers that trip when ordinary combinations run, a panel with no open spaces, or a project that needs a new 240-volt circuit for an EV charger, a generator, a hot tub, or a workshop. One trip after a known overload can simply be the breaker doing its job. A pattern is the clue.
Is a warm panel a reason to upgrade?
A warm panel, a buzzing panel, or a burning smell is a reason to call, and to stop using that equipment. It may be a loose connection, which is a repair, or it may be a panel that needs replacement. Do not open the cover, and do not assume a bigger breaker is the fix. A bigger breaker on undersized wire is how wires overheat.
Lights dim when the air conditioner starts. Is that an upgrade?
Maybe, and maybe not. Dimming or brightening across the house when a motor starts can be a loose neutral, which is a repair and can be urgent, or it can be a service that is near its limit. The difference is a diagnosis. Do not take the panel apart to check it yourself.
We are adding an EV charger. Do we automatically need 200 amps?
Not automatically. Some panels have the capacity and the space. Some do not. A load look at the panel is the only honest answer. If the panel is full of tandem breakers, or it is a brand we will not add a charger to, the panel is part of the project.
What is the difference between more circuits and a bigger service?
More outlets on a new circuit fix a room that does not have enough places to plug in. A bigger service fixes a house whose main panel cannot carry the loads it already has, or the load you are about to add. They are often sold as the same job. They are not.
