What a $99 Whole-Home Electrical Safety Inspection Covers

The $99 whole-home electrical safety inspection is a licensed electrician's visit, not a second home inspection and not a city sign-off. Here is what it covers, what it cannot see, and how to book it.

Quick answer

The $99 whole-home electrical safety inspection is a licensed electrician's look at the panel, the wiring we can reach, and the devices that protect people. You get a plain-language summary. It is not a city inspection, and the $99 does not include repairs. Schedule it as a system assessment.

What we actually look at

  • The service panel, with the cover removed by the electrician: breaker brand, signs of heat, double-taps, open spaces, grounding and bonding, and the labeled service size.
  • Wiring we can reach in the attic, basement, crawl space, or garage: knob-and-tube, cloth-insulated cable, aluminum, open splices, and junctions without covers.
  • A sample of outlets and switches: hot-ground reverse, open ground, loose devices, and GFCI protection in kitchens, baths, the garage, and outdoors.
  • Things you already noticed: a warm switch, a breaker that trips, lights that dim, a panel that buzzes. Tell us before we start so we spend the time there.
  • A written summary, in normal language, of what we found and what we recommend doing now, later, or not at all.

The price of this visit is $99. That is the whole-home electrical safety inspection. It is the only price on this website. Repair, a panel change, or a rewire is a separate scope, explained before that work starts. This page does not list those prices.

What this visit is not

It is not the City of Olathe's inspection. A city inspection happens when a permit is pulled for electrical work. This visit is for you: a buyer, an owner, or someone who got a letter from an insurance company and wants a licensed electrician to look.

It is not a promise that a 1960 house meets the code book published this year. Older houses are generally allowed to stay as they were legally built, until you change them. We will tell you what is deteriorated, what is obsolete, and what would have to change if you remodel. We will not hand you a list of every update that a brand-new house would have.

It is not a look inside every finished wall. If the cable is buried in plaster and there is no symptom, we do not open the wall to sightsee. If a symptom points at a spot, that spot is part of the visit.

A home inspector's electrical notes are a screen, not a diagnosis. Home inspectors are usually not licensed electricians, and they often do not take the panel cover off. If their report says Federal Pacific, Zinsco, knob-and-tube, or aluminum, this is the visit that confirms it.

Who books it

Buyers of older Olathe and Johnson County houses. Owners who have never had the panel cover off. Anyone who wants to know whether cloth wiring, knob-and-tube, or an obsolete panel is actually in the house before they plan a remodel. If you already know you need a panel replacement or a rewire, you can skip ahead to those services. If you do not know, the $99 inspection is the assessment.

Guides that match what we often find: Stab-Lok panels, Zinsco panels, knob-and-tube, cloth-insulated wiring, and signs of a panel upgrade. The service page is electrical safety inspection. Local details: electrician in Olathe, KS.

Common Questions

Safety inspection questions

What does the $99 whole-home electrical safety inspection include?
A licensed electrician looks at the panel with the cover off, at wiring that can be reached in the attic, basement, crawl space, or garage, and at a sample of outlets, switches, and GFCI protection. You get a plain-language summary of what we found and what we recommend. It is not a city inspection, and it is not a promise that an older house meets every rule in today's code.
Is $99 the price of repairs too?
No. The $99 is the inspection. If something needs repair, a panel change, or a rewire, that is a separate scope, explained before any of that work starts. This page does not list those prices.
How is this different from a home inspector's visit?
A home inspector does a visual pass and is usually not a licensed electrician. This visit is an electrician opening the panel, testing devices, and telling you what the wiring we can see is actually doing. Wires hidden inside finished walls stay hidden unless there is a reason to open something.
Should I book this before buying a house in Johnson County?
It is a good idea when the house is older, when the seller's report mentions the panel or the wiring, or when you want a licensed electrician's notes before you negotiate. Bring any inspection report you already have.
What should I do to get ready?
Clear a path to the panel, the attic access, and the electrical equipment in the garage or basement. You do not need to remove the panel cover. If a breaker is already off because of a burning smell, leave it off.
