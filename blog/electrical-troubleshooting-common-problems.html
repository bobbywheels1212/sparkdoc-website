Electrical Troubleshooting: Common Problems Homeowners See

Breakers that trip, lights that flicker, outlets that go dead, and switches that run warm are the problems we hear about most. Here is what those symptoms usually mean, what is safe to try, and when the next step is an electrician rather than another reset.

Quick answer

Most of what homeowners notice falls into four buckets: a breaker that trips, lights that flicker, an outlet that is dead, and a switch or outlet that is warm. A few checks are safe. Anything inside the panel, past the breaker handle, is not a do-it-yourself job. When the simple reset does not hold, call an electrician.

Breakers that trip

A breaker is a safety device. It opens when a circuit draws too much, or when it sees a short. If you had a space heater and a hair dryer on one bathroom circuit, one trip can be normal. Unplug the extra load, move the handle fully off, then on.

Call when the breaker trips again on a normal load, trips the moment you reset it, trips with nothing plugged in, or feels hot. Leave it off. Do not replace it with a larger amp breaker. The number on the breaker is matched to the wire. A larger number does not make the wire safer. It lets the wire overheat. Do not take the panel cover off to "see which wire it is."

The longer article is breaker keeps tripping. If the pattern is the whole house, not one circuit, read signs you need a panel upgrade.

Flickering lights

One lamp that flickers when you touch it is often the bulb or the socket in the lamp. Start there. Flicker in a whole room, or in several rooms at once, is a connection problem or a supply problem until someone proves otherwise. Flicker that shows up when the air conditioner or another motor starts needs a diagnosis. It can be a loose neutral, which we treat as a real repair, not a quirk of an old house.

If the lights flicker and you also smell something hot, or you see sparks, stop using that circuit and call. Bulbs are not the project at that point. More detail: flickering lights.

Dead outlets

Two checks are reasonable before you call. First, GFCI receptacles. Kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoor outlets often share one GFCI, and the reset button may be in a different room than the dead outlet. Press RESET. Second, look at the breaker handles without removing the cover. A handle sitting in the middle is tripped. Push it firmly off, then on.

If RESET pops back out, or the breaker will not stay on, stop. That is a fault, not a sticky button. Do not open boxes along the wall to hunt for it. Outlets in the bath, garage, and outside that die together are a common pattern, written up in lost power in the bathroom, garage, or outside. A single dead outlet is covered in outlet not working.

Warm switches and outlets

A switch or outlet that is warm under your hand is usually a loose connection. Loose connections arc. Arcing makes heat. Heat is how old insulation and plastic boxes get into trouble. This is not a "tighten it this weekend" task, especially if the house has cloth-insulated or aluminum wiring. The device can come apart in your hand and leave you holding a brittle conductor.

Turn the breaker off if you can do that from the panel door. Unplug whatever is in the outlet. Do not keep using a warm device, a discolored plate, or a switch that crackles. Then call. If the panel itself is warm, or you smell burning at the panel, leave it alone and call. That guide is burning smell or sparks at the panel.

Common Questions

Troubleshooting questions

A breaker tripped once. Do I need an electrician?
If a known heavy load was running, a space heater plus a hair dryer on the same circuit for example, one trip can be the breaker doing its job. Unplug the extra load and reset it once. If it trips again with a normal load, trips immediately, or will not stay on, leave it off and call. Do not replace it with a larger breaker, and do not open the panel.
The lights flicker. What should I check before I call?
One lamp can be a bulb or a loose lamp plug. Flicker in a whole room, flicker in several rooms, or flicker when the air conditioner starts should be a call. If the flicker comes with a burning smell, heat, or sparks, stop using that circuit and call. The longer guide is the flickering-lights article.
An outlet is dead. What is safe for me to try?
Press RESET on GFCI receptacles in the kitchen, bathrooms, garage, and outside. Look at the breaker handles without removing the metal cover. A handle in the middle is tripped; move it fully off, then on. If the GFCI will not reset or the breaker will not stay on, stop and call.
A switch or outlet is warm. Can I tighten it?
No. Warm usually means a loose connection, and taking the device apart can make it worse, especially on old insulation. Turn the breaker off if you can do that from the panel door, unplug what you can, and call. Do not keep using a warm device.
When is it an emergency call instead of a scheduled assessment?
Sparks, a burning smell, a hot panel, smoke, a shock, or half the house going dark are reasons to call (913) 390-3780 and ask about the next opening. If there is fire or smoke you cannot control, leave and call 911 first. Hours are Monday through Friday, 8 to 5, and Saturday, 8 to 12.
