Olathe Homes and the 100A to 200A Jump

Plenty of Olathe houses still run on 100-amp service: older ranches, mid-century builds, and first-generation subdivisions that never saw an EV charger, heat pump, or second HVAC. That panel was fine when the biggest loads were a dryer and a window AC. Today a Level 2 charger, a kitchen remodel, or a finished basement pushes the same box past its limit. Breakers that trip when the microwave and AC run together, or a panel with no open slots, are the usual tell.

Most Olathe upgrades land on 200-amp service. That is the size that supports a home EV charger, modern HVAC, and room for future dedicated circuits. Upgrade vs. Replacement

A panel upgrade is about capacity: moving from 100A to 200A (or 400A) so the house can handle EV chargers, HVAC, additions, and modern appliance loads. A panel replacement is about the box itself: if the service is already sized right but the panel failed, was recalled, or is unsafe, we swap it at the same amperage. If you need both, we do both in one project. We will tell you which one you actually need.

Breakers that will not hold, odd outages, or a panel that is warm to the touch often start as troubleshooting, then we replace the box if that is what the diagnosis says.

When the Panel Needs Replacing, Not More Amps

A panel upgrade is for houses that have outgrown 100-amp service. Replacement is different: the box itself is the problem. It may still be 200A and still be unsafe. Spark Doc Electric will tell you which job you actually have.

  • Federal Pacific Stab-Lok, Zinsco, Pushmatic, or Challenger panel
  • Insurance company will not write or renew a policy until the panel is replaced
  • Burn marks, melted bus, rust, or water damage inside the can
  • Breakers that will not reset, or that feel loose on the bus
  • Buzzing, crackling, or a burning smell at the panel
  • Old fuse box that should be a modern breaker panel
  • Cover that will not sit flush, missing knockouts, or exposed bus
  • Home sale, inspection, or lender flagged the equipment

Burning smell or sparks at the box? That is not a wait-and-see item. Read burning smell or sparks at the panel, or call to ask about urgent help.

Common Questions

Panel Upgrade & Replacement FAQ

How do I know if I need a panel upgrade?
Signs include: frequently tripping breakers, a 100-amp panel with modern appliances, a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel, no room for new circuits, adding an EV charger or hot tub, or your insurance company flagging the panel.
How much does a panel upgrade cost in Kansas City?
Costs vary based on panel size, whether the service entrance needs upgrading, and permit fees. Spark Doc Electric provides upfront pricing before starting. Call (913) 390-3780 for an accurate quote.
Do I need a permit to upgrade my electrical panel?
Yes. Panel upgrades require an electrical permit in Kansas and Missouri and must be inspected. Spark Doc Electric handles permitting as part of the job. We pull the permit, complete the work, and schedule the inspection.
What is a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel and why is it dangerous?
These panels from the 1950s-1980s have defective breakers that frequently fail to trip under overload, creating a fire hazard. Many insurers won't cover homes with these panels. Replacement is strongly recommended.
How long does a panel upgrade take?
Most residential panel upgrades take 4-8 hours. Your power will be off during installation. We aim to have you fully powered up by the end of the day.
Can you replace my panel without increasing the amperage?
Yes. If 200-amp service already fits the house, we replace the failed equipment and land the existing circuits. If you are still on 100A and adding load, we will talk through a 100A to 200A upgrade instead.
What is the difference between panel replacement and a panel upgrade?
Replacement swaps a failed, unsafe, or obsolete panel, often at the same amperage. An upgrade increases capacity, typically 100A to 200A (or 400A) so the house can carry modern loads. Spark Doc Electric will not sell you more amps if the real issue is a bad can.
