When a GFCI Trips

A GFCI (ground-fault circuit interrupter) watches for tiny current leaks to ground and cuts power in milliseconds. That is what keeps a kitchen or bathroom outlet from becoming a shock hazard when something gets wet or a cord fails.

Trips happen for real reasons: moisture in the box, a bad appliance, a worn receptacle, or a wiring fault on the load side. Pressing RESET once is fine. If it will not hold, or it trips again as soon as you plug something in, stop cycling it and call.

Dead outlets on the same circuit?

One tripped GFCI often kills several outlets downstream. Before you assume wiring failed, see our dead outlet repair checklist, then book if RESET will not stick.

  • RESET button will not stay in
  • Trips when a specific appliance is plugged in
  • Trips in rain or after cleaning near the outlet
  • Multiple outlets dead after one GFCI trips
  • TEST button does nothing (device may be dead)

Failed GFCI vs. Wiring Problem

Not every trip means the receptacle is bad. Sometimes the GFCI is doing its job and the leak is further down the circuit: a junction box, a damaged cord, or moisture in an outdoor cover.

We test the device, check line vs. load wiring, and isolate whether the fault is the GFCI itself or something it is protecting. Swapping parts without that check is how people end up with a new outlet that still will not hold.

GFCI vs. AFCI (quick note)

Homeowners mix these up. GFCI = shock protection at outlets (or GFCI breakers). AFCI = arc-fault protection, usually at the breaker, meant to reduce fire risk from damaged wiring. If a breaker labeled AFCI or CAFCI is the one tripping, that is a different diagnosis than a bathroom GFCI. For breaker trips, see tripped breakers or electrical troubleshooting.

Where GFCIs Matter Most

Kitchen, Bath, Garage, and Outdoor

Kitchen

Countertop receptacles need GFCI protection. Trips often track to a coffee maker, toaster, or dishwasher on the protected circuit, or to a device that has simply worn out.

Bathroom

Steam and splash make bathrooms a classic GFCI location. If RESET will not hold after a shower, moisture in the box or a failed receptacle is common.

Garage

Garage receptacles protect tools and chargers. Cold, dust, and shared circuits mean nuisance trips that still need a real answer, not endless resets.

Outdoor

Exterior GFCIs take weather abuse. Cracked covers, wet boxes, and landscape lighting on the load side are frequent finds when an outdoor outlet goes dead.

Common Questions

GFCI Outlet FAQ

Why does my GFCI outlet keep tripping?
Moisture, a bad appliance, a worn receptacle, or a wiring fault on the protected circuit. If RESET will not hold, stop cycling it and have us test the device and the load side.
What is the difference between a GFCI and an AFCI?
GFCI protects people from shock. AFCI protects against arc faults that can start fires. Different jobs. An AFCI breaker trip is not the same diagnosis as a bathroom GFCI trip.
Where are GFCI outlets required?
Bathrooms, kitchen countertop receptacles, garages, outdoors, laundry areas, and other wet or damp locations under current code. Older homes often need updates when you remodel or sell.
Can I just replace a GFCI outlet myself?
Line and load are easy to reverse, and a miswired GFCI will not protect downstream outlets. If the fault is in the wiring, a new device alone will not fix it. Call (913) 390-3780.
How much does GFCI repair or install cost?
A simple replacement is usually affordable. Tracing a load-side fault or adding protection in multiple rooms costs more. We give upfront pricing before we start.
