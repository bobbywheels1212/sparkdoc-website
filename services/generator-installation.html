Generator Installation in Olathe and the Kansas City Metro

Three different ways to keep a house on during an outage: a Generac standby generator, a portable generator inlet with an interlock kit, and a home battery backup. They solve different problems. Generac standby generator installation

A standby generator sits outside, permanently, and starts on its own when the utility power drops. An automatic transfer switch disconnects the house from the utility and then connects the generator. When utility power returns, the switch moves the house back and the generator shuts down.

This is the fit when someone needs the lights, the refrigerator, a sump pump, or the furnace or air handler to come back without going outside in the dark. It also fits a house that is empty during the day. Natural gas or propane is the fuel. If you are on natural gas, the gas utility may need to look at the meter before the install can be finished. We do not control that schedule, and we will say so up front.

The generator can be set up for the whole house or for a chosen set of loads. Whole-house only works if the generator and the fuel supply are large enough for those loads. A smaller essential-load setup is often the honest answer. The assessment is where we do that math with you, looking at the panel rather than a catalog.

How a standby install goes

  • System assessment: panel, loads you care about, and a place for the unit that meets the manufacturer's clearances and the city's rules.
  • Fuel plan: natural gas, or propane with a tank location. We flag a meter upgrade if the gas utility is likely to require one.
  • Transfer switch and any panel work. If the panel cannot accept the equipment, a panel upgrade comes first.
  • Permit when the City of Olathe or your city requires one, then the inspection.
  • Startup, a test of the transfer, and a walkthrough of the exercise cycle so the unit is not a mystery later.

We install Generac standby generators for this offer. We do not describe ourselves as a factory dealer on this page. The work is the electrical installation: equipment, transfer switch, permit, and inspection.

Portable generator inlet and interlock kit

This is the setup for a portable generator you already own, or plan to own. An inlet box goes on the outside of the house. A cord from the generator plugs into that inlet. Inside the panel, an interlock kit makes it so the generator breaker and the main breaker cannot be on at the same time.

That interlock is the point. It is what stops the generator from backfeeding the utility lines. Plugging a generator into a dryer outlet does not do this, and it is not a safe shortcut. We will not set a house up that way.

Who it fits: a homeowner who can go outside, start the generator, and follow a labeled sequence on the panel. Who it does not fit: a house that needs power to return when nobody is home, or anyone who cannot safely be outside with the generator. Carbon monoxide is the other hard rule. The generator stays outside, away from doors, windows, and the garage. Never in the garage, never in a crawl space, never under a closed porch that traps exhaust.

How an inlet and interlock go in

  • We look at the panel brand and whether a listed interlock exists for that exact panel. Not every panel can take one.
  • You bring the generator model, if you already have it, so the inlet matches the plug and the generator's output.
  • We choose an inlet location with a short, sensible cord path and with the exhaust pointed away from the house.
  • We install the breaker, the interlock, and the inlet, and we label the sequence on the panel.
  • A permit is pulled when your city requires one for the new circuit or the interlock.

Running the generator after that is your job, and the label is there so you are not guessing. Installing the inlet and the interlock is our job. Do not remove the panel cover to try this yourself.

If the panel is Federal Pacific or Zinsco, we are not adding an interlock to it. Those panels get replaced. See Stab-Lok panels and Zinsco panels.

Home battery backup

A home battery backup, sometimes called battery standby, stores energy and switches selected loads over when the utility drops. There is no engine and no fuel. It is quiet, and the switchover is fast, which matters for a modem, a medical device, or a sump that you do not want to wait on.

It fits shorter outages and a defined set of essential loads. It is a poor fit if you need several days of whole-home power and you have no way to recharge. A battery does not make fuel. Once it is empty, it is empty until it can charge again. A long winter outage usually points back to a fueled standby generator.

Some batteries can charge from solar. We are an electrical contractor, not a solar company. If you already have solar, or you want the battery choice to leave that door open, say so at the assessment so the equipment matches. We will not pretend a battery is a generator, and we will not pretend a generator is quiet.

How a battery backup goes in

  • We list the loads you actually want: refrigeration, a furnace blower or air handler, a few lights, internet, a sump, medical equipment.
  • We check the panel for space and condition, and the wall or pad where the battery can live within the manufacturer's temperature guidance.
  • We tell you, plainly, if the runtime you want is more than a battery can honestly cover.
  • Install, labeling, and a permit when the city requires one.
  • A walkthrough of what is backed up and what is not, so a dark room during an outage is not a surprise.

Panel work, when it is required, is a panel upgrade or replacement, not a side note. A new 240-volt circuit for other equipment is a dedicated circuit.

Which one, in one pass

  • Generac standby if the power should return with nobody home, and you have natural gas or a place for propane.
  • Inlet and interlock if you will own a portable generator and you will start it yourself, outside.
  • Home battery if you want quiet backup of essential loads and your outages are usually short.

Common Questions

Generator installation questions

Which backup system fits my house?
It depends on whether you need the power to come back on by itself, whether you already own a portable generator, and how long you need to run. A Generac standby generator starts automatically and can carry the whole house or a chosen set of loads if the fuel supply and the panel can support it. A portable inlet with an interlock fits a homeowner who will go outside and start a portable generator. A home battery backup fits shorter outages and essential loads, without fuel or engine noise. A system assessment is how we choose.
Do I need a panel upgrade before a generator?
Sometimes. A standby generator needs a transfer switch and space in or next to the service. A portable inlet needs a breaker space and a panel that can take an interlock. If the panel is full, damaged, or a brand we will not add equipment to, the panel comes first. We will tell you that at the assessment, before any install is scheduled.
Is it safe to plug a generator into a dryer outlet?
No. Backfeeding through a dryer outlet can energize the house wiring and the utility lines. That puts line workers and your own equipment at risk. The safe connection is an inlet and an interlock, or a transfer switch, installed so the generator and the utility cannot feed the panel at the same time.
Can a home battery replace a Generac standby generator?
Not always. A battery is quiet and starts quickly, and it is a good fit for essential loads during a shorter outage. It does not make its own fuel. A long outage, or a house that needs to run large loads for days, usually points to a fueled standby generator. Some homes use a battery for the essentials and a generator plan for the rest. The assessment sorts that out.
Do you pull permits for generator installation in Olathe?
When the City of Olathe, or another city we work in, requires a permit for the transfer switch, the generator, or the new circuit, we pull it and meet the inspector. The address decides which office that is. Ask at the assessment.
What happens on a system assessment?
We look at the panel, talk through the loads you want during an outage, and check where a generator, an inlet, or a battery could sit.
